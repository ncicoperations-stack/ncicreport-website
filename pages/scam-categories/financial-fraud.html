UNDERSTANDING FINANCIAL FRAUD

What Is Financial Fraud?

Financial fraud involves the deliberate use of deception, misrepresentation, or unauthorized activity to obtain money, financial assets, or sensitive financial information from individuals, businesses, or organizations.

Criminals use many different methods to commit financial fraud, including unauthorized banking transactions, investment fraud, payment fraud, fake invoices, account takeover, credit card fraud, and fraudulent loan or insurance schemes. These activities may occur online, over the telephone, through email, by text message, or in person.

Financial fraud may result in direct monetary losses, compromised bank accounts, damaged credit histories, unauthorized purchases, or stolen financial identities. Early recognition and prompt reporting can help reduce further losses and support intelligence efforts to identify broader criminal activity.

Preserving transaction records, communications, account statements, and payment confirmations can significantly strengthen a financial fraud report and assist investigators in understanding how the fraud occurred.

WARNING SIGNS

Common Indicators of Financial Fraud

Financial fraud can occur gradually or without immediate notice. Recognizing these warning signs may help limit additional financial losses.

1

Unauthorized Transactions

Purchases, withdrawals, transfers, or payments you did not authorize may indicate fraudulent activity.

2

Unexpected Account Changes

Password changes, account lockouts, altered contact information, or unfamiliar login alerts should be investigated immediately.

3

Suspicious Payment Requests

Requests for urgent wire transfers, gift card payments, cryptocurrency, or unusual payment methods are common indicators of fraud.

4

Unexpected Financial Documents

Bills, invoices, loan notices, or account statements for unfamiliar services may indicate unauthorized financial activity.

5

Requests for Confidential Information

Emails, text messages, or phone calls requesting banking credentials, one-time verification codes, or payment information should always be verified independently.

HOW IT HAPPENS

Common Methods Used in Financial Fraud

Financial fraud can occur through digital platforms, traditional banking systems, payment services, or social engineering. Understanding common techniques can help individuals and organizations recognize suspicious activity before significant losses occur.

Banking Fraud

Criminals may gain unauthorized access to bank accounts through stolen credentials, phishing attacks, malware, or compromised devices, allowing them to transfer funds or make unauthorized purchases.

Credit Card Fraud

Stolen card details may be used for online purchases, counterfeit cards, or unauthorized recurring payments. Fraudsters often obtain payment information through data breaches, skimming devices, or phishing campaigns.

Investment & Business Fraud

Fraudsters may promote fake investment opportunities, guaranteed high returns, or impersonate legitimate financial institutions to convince victims to transfer money.

Invoice & Payment Fraud

Businesses and individuals may receive fraudulent invoices or payment requests that appear legitimate, encouraging payment to criminal-controlled accounts.

Account Takeover

Once login credentials are compromised, criminals may change passwords, update account information, and gain long-term control of financial accounts.

WHO MAY BE TARGETED

Who Can Be Affected?

Financial fraud affects individuals, businesses, charities, government organizations, and financial institutions. Criminals often target those with valuable financial information or access to funds.

Individuals

Personal banking, online shopping, digital wallets, and payment accounts are frequent targets for financial fraud.

Businesses

Organizations may experience invoice fraud, payroll fraud, business email compromise, and unauthorized financial transactions.

Older Adults

Criminals frequently target older adults through investment scams, impersonation schemes, and fraudulent financial requests.

Online Consumers

People making online purchases or using digital payment platforms may encounter fraudulent merchants, fake payment requests, or stolen payment credentials.

PREVENTION

Protecting Yourself Against Financial Fraud

Strong financial security practices and regular account monitoring can significantly reduce the likelihood of becoming a victim of financial fraud.

  • Review bank and credit card statements regularly for unauthorized transactions.
  • Enable multi-factor authentication for financial accounts whenever available.
  • Never share banking credentials, verification codes, or PINs with anyone.
  • Verify payment requests independently before transferring money.
  • Use strong, unique passwords for all financial services and update them regularly.
  • Keep computers, mobile devices, and banking apps updated with the latest security patches.
  • Be cautious of unsolicited investment offers, urgent payment requests, or promises of guaranteed financial returns.
  • Immediately report suspicious financial activity to your financial institution and preserve all available evidence.
IF YOU SUSPECT FINANCIAL FRAUD

What To Do Immediately

Acting quickly may reduce financial losses, secure your accounts, and preserve valuable evidence for reporting and analysis.

1

Contact Your Financial Institution

Notify your bank, payment provider, or credit card issuer immediately if you notice suspicious activity or unauthorized transactions.

2

Secure Your Accounts

Change passwords, revoke unauthorized access, enable multi-factor authentication, and review recent account activity.

3

Preserve Evidence

Save transaction records, invoices, emails, payment confirmations, screenshots, and any communications related to the incident.

4

Monitor Your Finances

Continue monitoring your accounts, payment methods, and credit reports for additional unauthorized activity.

5

Submit a Financial Fraud Report

Submit a detailed report through the National Cyber Intelligence Center and include all available supporting evidence.

SUPPORTING EVIDENCE

Information That Can Strengthen Your Report

  • Bank statements showing unauthorized transactions.
  • Credit card statements and payment confirmations.
  • Receipts and invoices.
  • Email correspondence with suspected fraudsters.
  • SMS messages and call records.
  • Screenshots of fraudulent websites or advertisements.
  • Cryptocurrency wallet addresses or transaction IDs (if applicable).
  • Names, email addresses, phone numbers, or account details involved.
  • Dates, times, and descriptions of suspicious activity.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS

Financial Fraud FAQs

Should I report attempted financial fraud?

Yes. Attempted fraud provides valuable intelligence and may help identify criminal methods before additional victims are affected.

Can I submit additional evidence later?

Yes. Keep your NCIC reference number available when providing additional documentation or updates.

What evidence should I include?

Include financial records, payment confirmations, screenshots, communications, invoices, and any other documents related to the incident.

Does reporting guarantee recovery of funds?

No. Reporting supports cyber intelligence and may assist investigative efforts, but it does not guarantee recovery of financial losses.
